Rating system

Elo Ledger

One number for every footballer, built from what they actually did and weighed in the open.

A custom Elo-style rating system for football, built as a multi-tab app. I have taken it through several versions, and one of them ran a parallel MMA model on real UFC fight data.

Inputs

What goes into a rating

Seven inputs, each with its own weight in the points framework.

  • GoalsThe base of an attacker's score.
  • AssistsCredit for creating, not only finishing.
  • Clean sheetsSo defenders and keepers can rank alongside forwards.
  • Trophies, weighted by prestigeA league title or a Champions League counts for more than a minor cup.
  • Individual awardsRecognition from outside the team.
  • Era multipliersPuts players from different periods in one table.
  • Manager efficiency indexExtends the same framework to the dugout.

Simulation

Open Weight Division

Five years of a fictional MMA promotion, simulated in the browser, with every fight replayed in 3D.

Build a roster and watch 260 weeks play out: rankings, title fights, belts, betting odds, judges' scorecards, injuries, boxing crossovers and wrestling side quests. The same seed always gives the same five years.

Engine

How a fight is decided

Every bout is simulated in 20-second exchanges. Each fighter's stats, skills and traits turn into fight-night numbers, and every strike, takedown, knockdown and submission attempt is logged.

The fight videos replay that log. At 30 times speed a five-minute round lasts ten seconds, with live health and gas bars, strike counts and a broadcast camera that cuts closer for knockdowns and the ground game.

It runs as a static site with three.js for the 3D, has offline and online modes, and is packaged as a desktop app.

Game design

Poker Chess

Bet your Queen. Win the pieces, then win the board.

Two players play five hands of heads-up Texas Hold'em, betting chess pieces instead of chips. Pieces you win become yours. Then the felt folds open into a chessboard and the armies you finished with play chess for the match. I have written the full rulebook and a ten-phase build plan, and I am building it now.

Flow

How a match runs

  1. Five hands of No-Limit Hold'em. The blinds rise each hand.
  2. Pieces you win change to your colour. The King can never be bet.
  3. Both players secretly deploy whatever army they have left.
  4. Chess decides the match, and the result moves your Elo rating.

RoadSense

Which Southampton streets to treat this week, before the potholes form.

Built in 24 hours with Nilen Rao and Aryan Patel at SotonShip26, Southampton City track. It scores every council road on pothole risk from heavy-vehicle load, bus routes, flood areas, freeze-thaw weather and past reports, then ranks a weekly prevention plan for a highways officer to approve. I built the product: the API, the officer dashboard and the report triage.
